01 / Conclusion exécutive

EBS doit donner la priorité à l’Afrique subsaharienne francophone.

Les marchés prioritaires à court terme sont le Cameroun, la Côte d’Ivoire, le Sénégal et la République démocratique du Congo, suivis du Mali, du Burkina Faso, du Bénin et du Togo. The evidence combines Tunisia’s international-student footprint, African mobility patterns, higher-education orientation ecosystems, AI demand signals and current 2026 activity around Tunisia as an education destination.

9,047 foreign students in Tunisia

2023–2024 total across public and private higher education.

7,036 African students

Africa represented 77.8% of foreign students in Tunisia.

5,524 Sub-Saharan students

The official Study in Tunisia platform reports 5,524 Sub-Saharan students and 76 nationalities.

Interprétation stratégique : Tunisia already has the market. EBS does not need to convince Africa that Tunisia exists; it needs to win visibility for EBS + business + AI + employability + international pathways inside the markets already sending students to Tunisia.
02 / Tunisie d’abord — audit SEO critique

EBS has the assets to dominate its category, but the website is not yet extracting their full SEO value.

The current site has a powerful proposition — state approval, 190+ international certifications, AI integrated across programmes, 14 international academic partners, corporate partnerships and a real Tunis campus. The biggest gains now come from technical consistency, multilingual quality, entity consolidation, programme-level topical authority and stronger Tunisian third-party citations.

9.0/10 proposition strength

AI + certifications + international mobility + employability is a differentiated combination.

6.5/10 SEO execution

Good content assets, but important crawl/content consistency issues remain.

9.5/10 AEO/GEO potential

EBS has first-party facts, programmes, partners, campus and experts that answer engines can use.

05 / African evidence signals

Pourquoi ces marchés sont crédibles

Cameroon & Côte d’Ivoire: strongest business/AI proxy

Campus France reports that in 2023–24 Cameroon accounted for 2,761 foreign students in French business schools and 2,059 in engineering schools; Côte d’Ivoire accounted for 2,747 and 826 respectively. Both markets also show strong five-year growth in these disciplines.

This is not a direct Tunisia search-volume measure. It is a strong proxy for appetite for business, technology and internationally mobile higher education.

Senegal: large education discovery market

CampusEN lists 1,286 available programs, 400+ private universities and 10 public universities. SenCampus reports 388 institutions and 175K students accompanied, with free institution listing.

DR Congo: direct 2026 Tunisia signal

Tunisian official media reported in April 2026 that Tunisia wants to position itself as a preferred educational destination for Congolese students. This makes DRC a particularly timely market for an EBS campaign.

Mali: direct Tunisia admissions signal

Mali’s Directorate General of Higher Education published the list of Malian scholarship and non-scholarship students accepted by Tunisian universities for 2024–25. This is direct evidence of active Tunisia-bound student mobility.

07 / Stratégie des parcours internationaux

Vendre le parcours « EBS → international ».

France is the strongest external mobility story

France remains a major destination for African students. In 2023–24, Senegal, Côte d’Ivoire and Cameroon were among the top African origins of international students in France; business and engineering are particularly relevant to EBS.

Build country-specific pathway pages

For every priority country, publish: “Study in Tunisia from [country]”, “EBS admission for [country]”, “EBS AI programs for [country]”, “EBS → France after graduation”, and a transparent international-student checklist.

EBS advantage: EBS already markets international pathways and has published material describing international progression, co-diplomation, France opportunities, English-language options and partner-school pathways. Turn those assets into structured, country-specific proof pages.

10 / Plan d’exécution sur 12 mois

De zéro à un véritable moteur d’acquisition SEO africain.

01

Weeks 1–2 — Foundation

Fix multilingual indexing, canonical/hreflang, Organization/EducationalOrganization schema, zero counters, NAP consistency, program URLs, XML sitemap, Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

02

Weeks 2–6 — Program authority

Make every Bachelor/Master page a complete answer page: curriculum, certifications, careers, international routes, tuition, admission, FAQs, faculty and evidence.

03

Weeks 4–10 — African landing pages

Launch Cameroon, Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal and DRC first. Then Mali, Burkina Faso, Benin and Togo. Each page must be genuinely localized — not doorway pages.

04

Month 2–4 — Discovery backlinks

Secure 5–10 relevant free listings per country, then 1–2 high-quality editorial/PR placements per priority country. Measure referral sessions and assisted conversions.

05

Month 3–6 — AI authority

Publish professor-led AI explainers, student projects, AI career reports, AI events and original data. Make EBS an information source that AI engines can cite.

06

Month 6–12 — International brand moat

Build alumni pages, faculty entities, partner pages, international pathway proof, graduate outcomes, annual reports and digital PR. Track AI visibility in Google AI features, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.

09 / Recommandation finale

Devenir la référence sur « Étudier en Tunisie depuis l’Afrique », puis sur « IA + Tunisie + carrière internationale ».

EBS has a stronger underlying proposition than a normal university SEO campaign. The winning narrative is: European-style higher education in Tunisia + AI skills + international certifications + career outcomes + pathways abroad.

First 4 markets: Cameroon → Côte d’Ivoire → Senegal → DR Congo.

Second wave: Mali → Burkina Faso → Benin → Togo.

Content languages: French first, English for international/tech intent, Arabic where it supports cross-border African search demand.

Primary KPI: qualified international applications, not backlinks.
