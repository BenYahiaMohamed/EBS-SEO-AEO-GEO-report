01 / Conclusion exécutive

EBS doit donner la priorité à l’Afrique subsaharienne francophone.

Les marchés prioritaires à court terme sont le Cameroun, la Côte d’Ivoire, le Sénégal et la République démocratique du Congo, suivis du Mali, du Burkina Faso, du Bénin et du Togo. Les données combinent Tunisia’s international-student footprint, africain mobility patterns, higher-education orientation ecosystems, Demande en intelligence artificielle signaux and current 2026 activity around Tunisia as an education destination.

9,047 étudiants étrangers in Tunisia

2023–2024 total across public and private higher education.

7,036 étudiants africains

Africa represented 77.8% of étudiants étrangers in Tunisia.

5,524 étudiants d’Afrique subsaharienne

The official Étudier en Tunisie platform reports 5,524 étudiants d’Afrique subsaharienne and 76 nationalities.

Interprétation stratégique : La Tunisie dispose déjà du marché. EBS does not need to convince Africa that Tunisia exists; it needs to win visibility for EBS + commerce + IA + employability + parcours internationals inside the markets already sending students to Tunisia.
02 / Tunisie d’abord — audit SEO critique

EBS has the assets to dominate its category, but the website is not yet extracting their full SEO value.

The current site has a powerful proposition — state approval, 190+ international certifications, IA integrated across programmes, 14 international academic partners, corporate partnerships and a real Tunis campus. The biggest gains now come from technical consistency, multilingual quality, entity consolidation, programme-level topical authority and stronger Tunisian third-party citations.

9.0/10 proposition strength

IA + certifications + international mobility + employability is a differentiated combination.

6.5/10 SEO execution

Good content assets, but important crawl/content consistency issues remain.

9.5/10 AEO/GEO potential

EBS has first-party facts, programmes, partners, campus and experts that answer engines can use.

05 / Indicateurs de marché africains

Pourquoi ces marchés sont crédibles

Cameroon & Côte d’Ivoire: indicateur fort pour le commerce et l’IA

Campus France reports that in 2023–24 Cameroon accounted for 2,761 étudiants étrangers in French école de commerces and 2,059 in engineering schools; Côte d’Ivoire accounted for 2,747 and 826 respectively. Both markets also show strong five-year growth in these disciplines.

Il ne s’agit pas d’un volume de recherche direct pour la Tunisie. It is a strong proxy for appetite for commerce, technology and internationally mobile higher education.

Senegal: large education discovery market

CampusEN lists 1,286 available programs, 400+ private universities and 10 public universities. SenCampus reports 388 institutions and 175K students accompanied, with free institution listing.

DR Congo: signal direct de 2026 concernant la Tunisie

Tunisian official media reported in April 2026 that Tunisia wants to position itself as a preferred educational destination for Congolese students. This makes DRC a particularly timely market for an EBS campaign.

Mali: signal direct d’admissions vers la Tunisie

Mali’s Directorate General of Élevéer Education published the list of Malian scholarship and non-scholarship students accepted by Tunisian universities for 2024–25. This is direct evidence of active Tunisia-bound student mobility.

07 / Stratégie des parcours internationaux

Vendre le parcours « EBS → international ».

La France est le principal axe de mobilité internationale

France remains a major destination for étudiants africains. In 2023–24, Senegal, Côte d’Ivoire and Cameroon were among the top africain origins of international students in France; commerce and engineering are particularly relevant to EBS.

Créer des pages de parcours propres à chaque pays

For every priority country, publish: “Étudier en Tunisie depuis [country]”, “EBS admission for [country]”, “EBS IA programs for [country]”, “EBS → France after graduation”, and a transparent international-student checklist.

Avantage EBS : EBS already markets parcours internationals and has published material describing international progression, co-diplomation, France opportunities, English-language options and partner-school pathways. Turn those assets into structured, country-specific proof pages.

10 / Plan d’exécution sur 12 mois

De zéro à un véritable moteur d’acquisition SEO africain.

01

Semaines 1–2 — Fondations

Fix multilingual indexing, canonical/hreflang, Organization/EducationalOrganization schema, zero counters, NAP consistency, program URLs, XML sitemap, Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

02

Semaines 2–6 — Autorité des formations

Make every Bachelor/Master page a complete answer page: curriculum, certifications, carrières, international routes, tuition, admission, FAQs, faculty and evidence.

03

Semaines 4–10 — Pages d’atterrissage africaines

Launch Cameroon, Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal and DRC first. Then Mali, Burkina Faso, Benin and Togo. Each page must be genuinely localized — not doorway pages.

04

Mois 2–4 — Acquisition de liens et visibilité

Secure 5–10 relevant free listings per country, then 1–2 high-quality editorial/PR placements per priority country. Measure referral sessions and assisted conversions.

05

Mois 3–6 — Autorité sur l’IA

Publish professor-led IA explainers, student projects, IA carrière reports, événements sur l’IA and original data. Make EBS an information source that IA engines can cite.

06

Mois 6–12 — Renforcement de la marque internationale

Construire alumni pages, faculty entities, partner pages, parcours international proof, graduate outcomes, annual reports and digital PR. Track IA visibility in Google IA features, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.

09 / Recommandation finale

Devenir la référence sur « Étudier en Tunisie depuis l’Afrique », puis sur « IA + Tunisie + carrière internationale ».

EBS has a proposition de fond plus forte qu’une campagne SEO universitaire classique. The winning narrative is: Enseignement supérieur de qualité internationale en Tunisie + IA skills + international certifications + carrière outcomes + pathways abroad.

4 premiers marchés : Cameroon → Côte d’Ivoire → Senegal → DR Congo.

Deuxième vague : Mali → Burkina Faso → Benin → Togo.

Langues des contenus : French first, English for international/tech intent, Arabic where it supports cross-border africain search demand.

Principal KPI: candidatures internationales qualifiées, et non le nombre de liens.
